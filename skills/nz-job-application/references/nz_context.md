# New Zealand job market context

Use this as background. The user's own `eligibility.md` and the job advert
always win over anything here. Do not state visa rules as facts in an
application; use only the wording the user recorded from their own visa.

## Work rights
- Many adverts ask candidates to have the right to work in NZ. Some roles
  (for example some government, defence or security-cleared roles) require NZ
  citizenship or residency. Treat this as a hard gate only when the advert says so.
- International students' work conditions depend on their own visa. Always read
  the conditions from `eligibility.md`. Never assume hours or dates.
- On the CV, show work rights in one short, clear line under the contact
  details, using the user's recorded wording.
- If a role's hours or dates clash with the user's recorded work conditions,
  that is an eligibility issue (UNCLEAR or FAIL), not a scoring issue.

## CV conventions in NZ
- Usually 2 pages, maximum 3 for experienced candidates.
- No photo, date of birth, age, marital status, nationality or religion.
- Reverse chronological order. Clear section headings.
- Plain, ATS-friendly layout: one column, no tables for layout, no text boxes.
- NZ English spelling (organise, analyse, programme for courses).
- "Referees available on request" is common; never list referees without the
  user's permission.

## Cover letter conventions
- One page. Addressed to a named person if the advert or a confirmed source gives one,
  otherwise "Kia ora" or "Dear Hiring Manager".
- Specific to the company: one confirmed, recent signal about the organisation.
- Plain, direct, warm. Avoid overselling and avoid generic AI phrases.
- A Te Reo Māori greeting such as "Kia ora" is common and fine; do not overuse Te Reo.

## Common job sites
SEEK, Trade Me Jobs, LinkedIn, employer careers pages, university career
portals. LinkedIn and some careers pages cannot be opened by tools: ask the
user to paste the text.
